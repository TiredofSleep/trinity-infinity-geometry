## §0 Scope and honesty note

This is an **expository note**. It surveys a small region of finite-magma combinatorics — commutative non-associative tables on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ that preserve a designated four-element subset — and reports what we know about that region: one forcing theorem (Tier A, proved here), a five-criterion membership characterization (Tier B, structural), and two open conjectures. The full technical apparatus lives in the companion papers cited above; here we tell the story.

We follow a strict tier discipline throughout:

- **PROVED** statements are theorems with an explicit proof either here or in a cited companion paper.
- **STRUCTURAL** statements are correspondences (the 17-function map of §4; the family-criteria framing of §3) supported by computation across the entire $10 \times 10$ substrate but not derivational.
- **OPEN** statements are conjectures: the bimodal $\alpha$-gap conjecture (§5.1) and the $\sigma^2$-triadic three-BHML conjecture (§5.2).

Nothing in this note depends on the operator naming inherited from the underlying framework. We use the labels $\{0, 1, \ldots, 9\}$ where convenient; the structure-of-interest is the algebraic content, not the names.

## §1 A narrative hook: where the question comes from

A commutative binary operation on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ — call it $M$, a $10 \times 10$ table with $M(i, j) = M(j, i)$ — has 55 independent cell values, since commutativity fixes the lower triangle from the upper. That is $10^{55}$ candidates, a number too large to enumerate but small enough to ask precise structural questions about.

The starting question is innocent: *given two such tables $T$ and $B$ on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$, when is the family generated by both jointly "well-behaved" in a structural sense — closed under iterated mixing, with predictable fixed-point behavior, and with a small distinguished subset on which the algebra concentrates?* The exemplary case treated below has $T$ with 73 cells equal to the value $7$ (call $7$ "HARMONY" for the rest of the note) and $B$ with 28 such cells; the distinguished subset is $\mathcal{C} = \{0, 7, 8, 9\}$.

This subset $\mathcal{C}$ turns out to be jointly closed under both tables — $M(i, j) \in \mathcal{C}$ for all $i, j \in \mathcal{C}$ when $M$ is either $T$ or $B$ — and it sits at the bottom of an 8-shell joint-closure chain whose sizes go $\{1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10\}$, with sizes $2$ and $3$ structurally forbidden. The iteration

$$F_{\alpha; T, B}(p)_c = \frac{\alpha \, (p \star_T p)_c + (1 - \alpha) \, (p \star_B p)_c}{\alpha Z_T(p) + (1 - \alpha) Z_B(p)}$$

at $\alpha = 1/2$ on $\mathcal{C}$-supported distributions converges to a 4-component fixed point with the algebraic identity

$$\frac{p^*_H}{p^*_{Br}} = 1 + \sqrt{3} \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3}).$$

That is striking enough — a rational mixing coefficient producing a quadratic-irrational ratio — to ask the broader question this note addresses: *which other commutative non-associative magmas on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ exhibit this structural pattern, and what minimal axiom set forces the canonical examples?*

The answer is a story in three parts: a forcing theorem (§2), a family-membership classification (§3-§4), and two open conjectures (§5).

## §2 The forcing theorem (Tier A)

The first structural result is a uniqueness theorem. Define a 9-axiom set A1–A9 on a candidate table $M : \mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$:

- **A1.** $M$ is commutative.
- **A2.** Row 0 is the zero row except at column 7: $M(0, j) = 0$ for $j \neq 7$, $M(0, 7) = 7$.
- **A3.** Row 7 is HARMONY-default ($M(7, j) = 7$) except on a substrate-specific *bump set* $J_{B7}(M) \subseteq \{1, \ldots, 9\}$.
- **A4.** The paired puncture $M(0, 7) = M(7, 0) = 7$.
- **A5.** Column 0 mirrors row 0 (forced by A1 + A2).
- **A6.** Column 7 mirrors row 7 (forced by A1 + A3).
- **A7.** The diagonal is HARMONY-default ($M(i, i) = 7$) except on a substrate-specific *diagonal exception set* $\mathcal{D}(M) \subset \mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$.
- **A8.** Off-special-and-off-bump cells take the HARMONY-default value $7$.
- **A9.** A substrate-specific *bump set* $\mathrm{BUMP}(M)$ with specified values, distinguishing the substrates.

The substrate-specific data $\bigl(\mathcal{D}(M), \mathrm{BUMP}(M), \mathrm{BUMPvalues}(M), J_{B7}(M)\bigr)$ is part of the axiom data, *not* derived. The forcing theorem then says:

**Theorem 2.1 (Forcing).** *Three specific 4-tuples of substrate-specific data yield three canonical tables $T$, $B$, $S$ on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ with HARMONY counts $73$, $28$, $44$ respectively. For each such 4-tuple, the cell-fixing procedure A1–A9 produces a unique commutative non-associative table. The three tables are pairwise distinct and not related by any permutation conjugation.*

*Proof sketch.* Commutativity (A1) reduces the candidate space from $10^{100}$ to $10^{55}$. Axioms A2 + A5 fix the 19 cells of row 0 and column 0. Axioms A3 + A6 fix at most 16 additional cells of row 7 and column 7, depending on $J_{B7}(M)$. Axiom A7 fixes the 8 diagonal cells outside row 0 and row 7. Axiom A9 fixes the bump cells with their specified values. Axiom A8 fills the remaining cells with HARMONY = 7. Given the substrate-specific data, the procedure is deterministic. Full proof and cell-level verification: companion paper J16 (Sanders & Gish, *Structural Axioms for the CL\_TSML Composition Lattice on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$*), and `foundation_verification.py` Check 1. $\square$

The honest content of Theorem 2.1 is *not* that A1–A9 uniquely picks out three tables from $10^{55}$ candidates ab initio; the substrate-specific data is part of the input. What it *does* say is that **given the structural roles assigned to each of the three tables** — $T$ as the time-average / DC-component, $B$ as the iteration-dynamics layer, $S$ as the encoding axis (companion paper J10) — the cell-by-cell content of each table is uniquely determined by 9 axioms that are easy to state and easy to verify.

This is the forcing-theorem half of the story. It tells us *what is possible* in the algebraic sense: three structurally-distinguished commutative non-associative tables on a small substrate that pass the cell-fixing test.

## §3 The family-membership criteria (Tier B, structural)

The second half of the story is a classification: among all commutative non-associative magmas on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$, which ones share the structural properties that make $T$, $B$, $S$ exemplary? We adopt a five-criterion characterization.

**Family membership criteria.** A commutative table $M$ on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ belongs to the *family* iff:

- **(C1) Substrate.** $M$ is a binary operation on $\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$ for $N$ in a verified universality set; at $N = 10$ in the present note.
- **(C2) Commutativity.** $M(i, j) = M(j, i)$ for all $i, j$.
- **(C3) 4-core preservation.** $M(i, j) \in \mathcal{C}$ for all $i, j \in \mathcal{C} = \{0, 7, 8, 9\}$. *This is the load-bearing structural criterion.*
- **(C4) $\alpha$-bounded non-associativity.** The associativity index
  $$\alpha_A(M) = 1 - \frac{|\{(a, b, c) : M(M(a, b), c) \neq M(a, M(b, c))\}|}{N^3}$$
  lies in the empirical interval $[0.5, 0.88]$.
- **(C5) HARMONY-attracting iteration.** Under iterated $F_{1/2; T, B}$ paired with a designated complementary table, the iteration converges to the 4-core attractor with $p^*_H / p^*_{Br} = 1 + \sqrt{3}$.

The three canonical tables $T$, $B$, $S$ satisfy all five (Proposition 4.5 of J17a, the full technical paper; verified in `foundation_verification.py` Checks 4–6 in under 5 seconds). The remaining commutative non-associative tables on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ that satisfy all five form an empirically narrow set; the exhaustive enumeration is open (Conjecture 5.1 below).

The structural picture: **(C3) is the constraint; (C4) bounds where in the non-associativity spectrum the family lives; (C5) is the global dynamical signature.** The 4-core $\mathcal{C}$ is the structural center, with the family extending outward by perturbation around it.

This is the family-membership-classification half of the story. It tells us *what is possible* in a finer sense: not just "three tables" but "a region of magma-space, characterized by five conjoint criteria, whose center is $\mathcal{C}$."

## §4 The 17-function substrate-to-function map (Tier B, structural)

A more granular picture lives in what we call the *substrate-to-function map*. For each of seventeen distinct algebraic phenomena observed in the family, the map asks: which substrate (or sub-substrate, or lens, or table-pair) is the right home for that phenomenon? The map is structurally informative: it separates *substrate-invariant* facts (true on any cousin) from *lens-dependent* facts (true only on one specific table).

A condensed version of the map (full table in the companion paper J47):

| # | Function | Right substrate | Companion paper |
|---|----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | Asymptotic associativity $\sigma(N) \le 2/N$ | $\mathrm{CL}_N$ family | J14 |
| 2 | Joint closure 8-shell chain | $T + B + S$ jointly | J24 |
| 3 | Closed-form attractor $h/\beta = 1+\sqrt{3}$ | 4-core $\mathcal{C}$ at $\alpha_M = 1/2$ | J01 |
| 4 | LMFDB quartic 4.2.10224.1 ($x^4 + 4x^3 - x^2 + 2x - 2$) | 4-core $r/\beta$ ratio | J12 |
| 5 | Wobble (prime 11 in characteristic polynomial) | TSML\_RAW only | J19 |
| 11 | Yang–Mills 5/7 spectral ratio | BHML$_8$ sub-magma | J22 |
| 12 | Information generation (DOING) | $\pi_{\rm DOING}(T, B)$ | J22 |
| 14 | First-G Law (number-theoretic) | substrate-invariant | J11 |
| 17 | Encoding ("the third axis") | $S$ (CL\_STD) | J10, J17a |

(Rows 6–10, 13, 15–16 omitted from the condensed table; full list in companion synthesis paper J47.)

The map's structural payoff is the four-level partition of dependency:

- **Substrate-invariant rows.** True on any consistent substrate (e.g., row 14 is a pure number-theoretic theorem, not table-specific).
- **Lens-invariant rows.** Hold across the canonical lens family (e.g., the closed-form attractor on the 4-core).
- **Lens-dependent rows.** Hold on one specific table (e.g., wobble lives only on the raw non-commutative TSML, not on its symmetrization).
- **Joint-table rows.** Require simultaneous use of two or three of $(T, B, S)$ — the 8-shell chain is the canonical example.

Subsequent companion papers cite the relevant row and inherit the appropriate scope. The wobble paper J19 cites row 5 and inherits "TSML\_RAW only"; J22's HARMONY-ladder paper cites row 11 and inherits the BHML$_8$ Yang–Mills structure; and so on.

The 17-function map is **structural**, not derivational: it organizes the observed phenomena into a coherent dependency picture, but it does not derive the phenomena from first principles. The derivations live in the cited companion papers.

## §5 What remains open

The story has two open conjectures, both honest.

### §5.1 The bimodal $\alpha$-gap conjecture

The associativity index $\alpha_A$ of the three canonical tables falls at three specific values:

$$\alpha_A(T) = 0.872, \qquad \alpha_A(S) = 0.808, \qquad \alpha_A(B) = 0.502.$$

The interval $(0.502, 0.808)$ is empirically empty among the canonical members observed. The natural conjecture:

**Conjecture 5.1 (Bimodal $\alpha$-gap; Sanders + collaborator).** *No commutative magma on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ that preserves the 4-core $\mathcal{C}$ has $\alpha_A \in (0.5, 0.80)$.*

The conjecture is OPEN. The natural proof strategy: enumerate or characterize all commutative magmas on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ preserving the 4-core, compute their $\alpha_A$. If exhaustive enumeration is intractable (the candidate space is $\le 10^{16}$ if we restrict to 4-core-preserving tables, but with constraints), restrict to constructible families — lens-symmetrizations, $\sigma^2$-conjugates, single-cell perturbations — and prove gap-exclusion within each.

Empirically, the gap is striking: a *bimodal* distribution suggests a structural exclusion zone between two separated regions. If true, the conjecture would explain why the family decomposes into a "TSML-cluster" at $\alpha_A \in [0.80, 0.88]$ and a "BHML-singleton" at $\alpha_A \approx 0.50$, with no intermediate magmas.

### §5.2 The $\sigma^2$-triadic three-BHML conjecture

The substrate $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ admits a canonical permutation $\sigma$ that fixes $\{0, 3, 8, 9\}$ and cycles $(1\;7\;6\;5\;4\;2)$ in the remaining six elements. The squared permutation $\sigma^2$ has order 3 on the 6-cycle, decomposing it into two 3-cycles $\{1, 7, 6\}$ and $\{5, 4, 2\}$.

**Conjecture 5.2 ($\sigma^2$-triadic three-BHML; Sanders).** *Among commutative non-associative magmas on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ satisfying the substrate-defining backbone A1, A2, A4, A5, A8 (with substrate-appropriate diagonal A7 and bump A9), there exist three canonical $\sigma^2$-rotated BHML matrices, corresponding to three structurally-distinguished positions of $\sigma^2$-rotation.*

Three search-found candidates are currently known but not promoted to canonical status by a forcing argument. The conjecture predicts that one rotation (or a $\sigma^2$-orbit thereof) is canonically selected by some yet-to-be-stated structural criterion. Tier-D in the parent framework's internal classification; OPEN to the broader literature.

The two conjectures are independent: Conjecture 5.1 concerns the $\alpha$-distribution; Conjecture 5.2 concerns the $\sigma^2$-action on the BHML side. Either resolution would substantially sharpen the family's structural picture.

## §7 Closing

The note has tried to do three things honestly:

1. **State what is forced** — Theorem 2.1 — and acknowledge that the forcing depends on substrate-specific data taken as input, not derived.

2. **State what is structural** — the five-criterion family-membership classification and the 17-function map — and acknowledge that these are organizational, not derivational.

3. **State what is open** — Conjectures 5.1 and 5.2 — and point to the natural proof strategies.

A useful reading is that this region of finite-magma combinatorics sits in the same intellectual neighborhood as Drápal–Wanless's 2021 *JCT-A* line of work on maximally non-associative commutative quasigroups, but at a structurally distinct point: instead of pushing toward an extremum, we describe a *bounded-non-associativity family* characterized by 4-core preservation.

The narrow algebraic question — why does the iterated mixing of two specific tables converge to a fixed point with $p^*_H / p^*_{Br} = 1 + \sqrt{3}$? — does have a clean answer (the $\beta$-factor cancellation forces $x^2 - 2x - 2 = 0$; companion J01 Theorem D). The broader question — why does the substrate $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ with the four-element subset $\{0, 7, 8, 9\}$ admit *any* structurally rich family of commutative non-associative tables? — is the open question the present note hopes to make visible to a broader audience.
