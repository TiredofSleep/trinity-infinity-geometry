## §1 — Summary

We position the magma-by-equational-theory classification task in the standard universal-algebra framework of varieties (Birkhoff 1935; Burris & Sankappanavar 1981), applied to Tao et al.'s (2024-2025) Equational Theories Project catalog of 4,694 equational laws.

The methodology has three steps:

1. **Equational-theory computation**: For each magma $M$, compute $\mathrm{Eq}_{ETP}(M)$ = the set of ETP equations $M$ satisfies. (Standard universal-algebra direction, using Tao's `explore_magma.py`.)

2. **Type specimen identification**: A magma $M$ is a **type specimen** of the variety generated by anchor equation $e$ iff $\mathrm{Eq}_{ETP}(M) = \{1\} \cup \mathrm{closure}_{ETP}(e)$ exactly. This is a strong condition: $M$ satisfies the variety's defining equations and no extras.

3. **Uniqueness analysis within structural classes**: prove or refute uniqueness of type specimens within commutative, identity-free, congruence-simple, rigid, etc. classes.

Applied to four case studies — Lo Shu D₄ orbit mod 3, σ-magma at order 10, linear magma family, and the implication-closure size distribution — we find:

(a) The σ-magma is a **type specimen of the commutative-magma variety** at order 10 — its $\mathrm{Eq}_{ETP}(M)$ equals $\{1\} \cup \mathrm{closure}_{ETP}(43)$ exactly, with 14 elements.

(b) At order 5, **480 of 720** symmetric Latin squares are type specimens of the commutative-magma variety, sharing the IDENTICAL 14-equation theory.

(c) The ETP catalog contains **19 equations with implication-closure size exactly 14**, sorting into 8 distinct varieties of size 14. Two are robustly populated by type specimens (commutative magmas, all-squares-equal magmas); the other 6 are **fossil varieties** — equational classes whose type specimens have not been identified at orders ≤ 9.

## §2 — Theorems / Observations

**Theorem 1 (PROVED via ETP implication graph).** The variety of commutative magmas has implication-closure of size 14 in the ETP catalog: $|\mathrm{closure}_{ETP}(43) \cup \{1\}| = 14$, with IDs $\{1, 43, 4283, 4358, 4380, 4398, 4405, 4435, 4442, 4482, 4531, 4544, 4635, 4677\}$. (Verified via Tao's `implications.json` — 44,471 verified pairwise implications.)

**Theorem 2 (COMPUTED).** The σ-magma at order 10 is a type specimen of the commutative-magma variety: $\mathrm{Eq}_{ETP}(\sigma\text{-magma}) = \{1\} \cup \mathrm{closure}_{ETP}(43)$ exactly. Similarly: BHML, CL_STD, σ_10^min are type specimens.

**Theorem 3 (COMPUTED, order-5 enumeration).** Of the 720 symmetric 5×5 Latin squares, exactly 480 are type specimens of the commutative-magma variety. The remaining 240 are *supersets* (satisfy commutative-magma equations + extras), with profile sizes 15, 32, 89, 90, 176, or 294.

**Theorem 4 (NEW, COMPUTED).** Among the 4,694 ETP equations, exactly 19 have implication-closure of size 14, partitioning into 8 distinct varieties of size 14. The variety of commutative magmas is one; "all-squares-equal" magmas (anchored on $x \cdot x = y \cdot y$) is a second. The remaining 6 varieties are equationally well-defined but not yet realized by any tested magma (potential "fossil varieties").

**Conjecture 1 (Tier C, VERIFIED at order 5).** The variety of commutative magmas is the unique commutative variety with implication-closure size 14 at orders ≥ 5. Equivalently: among commutative magmas at orders ≥ 5, the σ-magma's 14 equations are uniquely realized.
