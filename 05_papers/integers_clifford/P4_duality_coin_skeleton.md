# P4 — One Duality Axis: High-Tc, Spin Liquids, and Strange Metals as a Single Coin

## Opening disclaimer (to appear FIRST in the paper, verbatim in spirit)

> *This paper proposes an organizing frame, not a mechanism. It does not solve high-
> temperature superconductivity, does not identify the cuprate pairing glue, and does
> not prove any material is a quantum spin liquid. It offers a single-axis
> classification of three open problems, rooted in one geometric property, with a
> checkable midpoint. Its value, if any, is in placing disparate phenomena on one axis
> coherently; it is to be judged by whether that placement constrains anything, and is
> presented with that limit stated up front.*

## Abstract (draft)

From the two projections of the Clifford cube (P2) — the square (four-fold, matter)
and the hexagon (three-fold, flow) — we observe that a single property distinguishes
them: **whether a clean two-fold duality exists.** The square lattice is bipartite
(two-colorable, self-dual, even-cycled); the honeycomb/triangular lattice is not
(odd-cycled, three-colorable, frustrated). This duality/non-duality is, in the
Clifford picture, the presence or absence of the mass term that couples the two
chiralities (P2, P3). We then note that three of the hardest open problems in
correlated matter fall on the two ends and the midpoint of this one axis:
**high-temperature superconductivity** (matter reaching for flow — pairing on a dual
lattice), **quantum spin liquids** (flow refusing matter — resonance on a non-dual
lattice), and **strange metals** (the midpoint — where the directional part of the
duality vanishes at the angle whose cos² = 1/3). Every invariant we examine comes out
oppositely for the two poles, and the midpoint has a measured realization (the cuprate
pseudogap). We stress throughout that this is a *classification with a testable
middle*, not an explanation.

## 1. The single root: duality present or absent [FORCED]

- **Square (matter):** 2-colorable (checkerboard), self-dual, even cycles → a clean
  A/B duality throughout. [FORCED — chromatic number 2, bipartite.]
- **Hex/triangular (flow):** not 2-colorable (contains odd cycles/triangles),
  3-colorable instead (a *triad*, not a duality), frustrated. [FORCED — chromatic
  number 3.]
- **In the Clifford picture (P2, P3):** duality active ⇔ the mass term couples the two
  chiralities ⇔ massive/matter; duality absent ⇔ chiralities decoupled ⇔
  massless/flow. So **the one root is: does a two-fold duality exist?**

## 2. The polarity table [FORCED invariants; NAMED physics]

| invariant | HEADS: matter/square/dual | TAILS: flow/hex/non-dual |
|---|---|---|
| lattice | square (bipartite) | hex (frustrated) |
| cycle parity | even | odd |
| mass (P3) | massive (chiralities coupled) | massless (decoupled) |
| pairing | Cooper pairs **bind** (k↑/−k↓) | RVB spins **resonate** [Anderson 1973] |
| gap | present (d-wave: nodes on diagonals) | gapless |
| flow path | channeled (diagonal nodes) | pervasive (isotropic) |
| direction | matter → flow (unlock) | flow ↛ matter (anti-lock) |

- Every invariant is opposite, and none is hand-assigned — each follows from the
  duality/non-duality root. [The consistency across independent invariants is the
  paper's main structural observation.]

## 3. How flow moves through matter: the d-wave nodes [FORCED + NAMED]

- The default square lattice (half-filled, interacting) is a Mott insulator/
  antiferromagnet — **frozen matter, no flow** [NAMED].
- Doping creates holes; pairing is **d-wave**: Δ(k) = Δ₀(cos kₓ − cos k_y).
- *Verification:* the gap is maximal along the axes (bonds) and **zero along the
  diagonals** (the nodes) — computed. Flow passes through the **diagonal nodes**, where
  the duality has zeros. [FORCED gap structure; d-wave is MEASURED in cuprates.]
- **The diagonals point toward the body diagonal (P2) — the flow (hex) axis.** So
  matter carries flow along the very directions that point toward the flow lens: flow
  leaks through the cracks (nodes) in matter's duality. [FORCED geometry + READING.]

## 4. The midpoint: the pseudogap, and the strange-metal edge [MEASURED + FRAME]

- **The testable middle.** If the two problems are poles of one axis, a tunable system
  should pass through a half-formed midpoint. The cuprate phase diagram —
  antiferromagnet (order/matter) → **pseudogap** → superconductor (flow), tuned by
  doping — provides one; the pseudogap shows *both* superconducting and competing-order
  fluctuations, as the midpoint reading requires [NAMED: Keimer et al. 2015]. This is
  the paper's one checkable prediction of the frame.
- **The strange metal as the edge — with a mechanism, not a resemblance.** The strange
  metal (linear-in-T resistivity, no quasiparticles, scale-invariant) sits at the
  crossover. In the Clifford geometry, the directional (rank-2, quadrupolar) part of
  any interaction — the *directional signature of the duality* — **vanishes at the
  angle whose cos² = 1/3** (54.74°, the P1/P2 half-angle), because that angle is the
  root of the second Legendre polynomial (3cos²θ − 1 = 0) [NAMED: this is the NMR
  magic-angle condition, Andrew–Bradbury–Eades 1959]. *Verification:* 3cos²(54.74°) − 1
  = 0 (computed). A state at this angle has **no directional order → no anisotropic gap
  → no quasiparticle**: the strange-metal signature. [The angle and the P₂ zero are
  FORCED; the identification of the strange metal with this geometric edge is a FRAME
  with a stated mechanism, superior to a bare "incommensurate" resemblance but still
  not a transport derivation.]

## 5. What is claimed and what is not [boundary — critical]

- **[FORCED]:** the duality/non-duality root; the opposite polarity of every invariant;
  the d-wave node structure; the P₂ zero at cos² = 1/3.
- **[MEASURED]:** graphene/hBN, d-wave cuprates, the pseudogap's dual fluctuations, the
  magic angle.
- **[FRAME, explicitly not a mechanism]:** that high-Tc, spin liquids, and strange
  metals *are* the two poles and edge of one duality axis. This **organizes**; it does
  **not** give the pairing glue, prove a spin liquid, or derive strange-metal transport.
- **[OPEN]:** whether a Fermi surface constrained to the P₂-zero geometry actually
  yields linear-in-T resistivity — a real calculation that would upgrade §4 from frame
  to result. Stated, not done.

## 6. Conclusion (draft)

A single geometric property — the presence or absence of a two-fold duality, which in
the Clifford picture is the presence or absence of the mass coupling — places three of
the hardest problems in correlated matter on one axis: high-Tc at the dual (matter)
pole reaching toward flow, spin liquids at the non-dual (flow) pole refusing matter,
and strange metals at the midpoint where the duality's directional part vanishes (the
cos² = 1/3 angle, the P₂ zero). Every invariant is oppositely signed for the two poles,
and the midpoint has a measured realization in the cuprate pseudogap. The paper offers
this as a coherent classification with a checkable middle — not as a mechanism — and
states plainly the calculation that would be required to make the strange-metal edge a
result rather than a frame. Whether the organization constrains the open physics is for
the specialist to judge; the paper's discipline is to draw that boundary itself.
