## J14 — Non-Associativity Decay in Binary Composition Tables over Z/NZ

**Verdict:** **Minor revision** — the σ(N) ≤ 2/N theorem is rigorously proved for squarefree N ≥ 3, the proof is short and elementary, and the verification script's outputs match the manuscript claims exactly. A few editorial sharpenings and one citation correction are needed before submission.

**Verification cross-check:** All three tabulated values in §5 (Verification table, line 596–605) reproduce exactly: σ·N³ ∈ {128, 1560, 86464} for N ∈ {10, 30, 210}, with N·σ(N) ∈ {1.28, 1.73, 1.961}. `verify_sigma_rate.py` runs all five sub-verifications (Echo lemma, E_h closed form, σ-bound, ε-bound, asymptotic) and the bound σ(N) < 2/N holds at every squarefree N tested (we re-ran on N ∈ {3, 5, …, 105}; all pass).

### MAJOR issues

1. **[Bibliography line 686] Drápal–Wanless citation is to a different article than the one J22 cites for the same author pair.** J14 cites *Maximally nonassociative quasigroups from finite fields*, JCTA **181** (2021) 105444. J22 cites *Maximally non-associative quasigroups*, JCTA **184** (2021) 105510. These appear to be two distinct papers; the referee request also mentioned only the latter. Pick one, verify, and use consistent bibliographic data across both manuscripts (or cite both with disambiguation). At minimum a JCT-A copy editor will catch the inconsistency.

2. **[§4 line 491, Case 3.B, $x=0$ subcase] The squarefree-forces-contradiction step is correct but slightly under-justified.** The claim $a^2 \equiv 0 \pmod N \Rightarrow a \equiv 0 \pmod N$ for squarefree N is true (by CRT $a \equiv 0$ mod each prime $p | N$, hence mod $N$), but the manuscript states it without proof and a referee may pause. One sentence ("for squarefree $N$, the kernel of the squaring map on $\Z/N\Z$ is trivial since $\Z/N\Z \cong \prod_{p|N} \mathbb F_p$") would close the gap.

### MINOR issues

1. **[§4 line 506–507, Case 3.B, $x \in \{1,…,N-2\}$ subcase] Counting argument is loose by design but could be sharpened.** The bound "at most $\varphi(N)$ non-associative triples" counts every ECHO pair $(a,b)$ with the outer site disagreeing; in fact the (3.B) hypothesis $(b,c) \notin$ ECHO further restricts $c$, and excluding the bad cases would let you push closer to the tight constant. Not required for the theorem, but flag in the OPEN list.

2. **[§3 Remark line 247–256] The empirical counts in the Echo-count remark omit an explicit sanity check at $N=2$.** Lemma 3.1 covers $N \ge 2$ but the showcase set starts at $N=3$. Add $N=2$: φ(2)=1, single solution $(0,0)$.

3. **[§5 verification, footer line 617–618] DOI is `10.5281/zenodo.18852047`** — same DOI cited in J22 and the J15 companion. For JCT-A this is fine, but verify the deposit actually contains the J14 verify script (not just the J22 manuscript) before submission.

4. **[§1 line 147–150] The long, unbroken sentence on lens-choice for $\CL_{10}$ runs ~60 words.** Split for readability; the J15 companion reference is fine, but the parenthetical "(sizes {1,4,5,6,7,8,9,10})" and "8 vs 7 chain" detail belongs in the companion, not §1.

### EDITORIAL

1. **[Title vs abstract]** The title says "binary composition tables" (plural), but the paper studies one family $\CL_N$ parametrized by N. Consider "binary composition table family" or simply "the family $\CL_N$" in the abstract opening. A pedant referee will ask.
2. **[Abstract line 81–82]** "the substitution $(a-1)(b-1) \equiv 1$" — this is the key trick. Move it into the first sentence ("the proof reduces ECHO-counting to unit-counting via the substitution …"). Currently buried after a long sentence.
3. **[§4 line 559–581, non-squarefree remark]** Excellent honest disclosure of the $N = 64$ exception; consider promoting to its own short subsection rather than a 22-line Remark, since it is the natural follow-up question.
4. **[§4 Lemma 3.X "Fibonacci's polynomial"]** Calling $b^2 + b - 1$ "Fibonacci's polynomial" is informal — it is the minimal polynomial of the golden ratio. Either keep the colloquial name (cite OEIS / Knuth Vol 1) or call it the *golden-ratio polynomial*.

### Journal-fit (JCT-A specific)

The paper is a clean fit. JCT-A regularly publishes finite-combinatorial identities of this flavor (cf. Drápal–Wanless 2021 on the opposite-pole problem $\sigma \to 1$; the present paper's $\sigma \to 0$ pole is genuinely complementary). The Lemma 3.X (E_h closed form via $b^2 + b - 1$ over CRT/QR) adds independent number-theoretic content that strengthens the submission beyond a single-theorem note. **JCT-A reviewers will expect** that you state the precise scope ("squarefree N ≥ 3") in both the title or abstract proper, that the asymptotic claim be supported by the explicit $\varphi(N) \le N - 1$ bound used to close the strict-inequality step (it is, line 541–544), and that the residual ε(N) ≤ 2φ(N) bound be derivable without computer assistance — which it is (the (3.A)–(3.D) classification is a hand proof). All three boxes are checked.

## J22 — The 70/71/72/73 HARMONY Ladder

**Verdict:** **Major revision** — at JCT-A standards the paper as written is not yet ready. The four numerical claims verify at machine precision, but (a) three of the four "structurally independent" rungs are not as independent as the title and §1 claim, (b) the "ECHO pairs" terminology inside the paper conflicts with the J14 companion's ECHO rule and inflates a coincidence into a structural statement, (c) the central 71-triple includes one ingredient (the Galois-discriminant rung) that depends on an unpublished companion paper to even be well-defined, and (d) several "rungs" are described as theorems but their proofs read as direct integer evaluation of a fixed numerical matrix that was not derived in the paper.

**Verification cross-check:** All five rungs in `harmony_ladder.py` reproduce exactly (HARM(T)=73, HARM(T₉)=71, |T⊕B|=71, det(B_YM)=70, disc(f)=−40896=−2⁶·3²·71). Sympy gives the integer 70 for the YM determinant; the float computation also rounds to 70 cleanly. The cross-check that the alternate factorization $-2^7·3·7·19 = -51072$ disagrees with $-40896$ is sound (and tactically useful for pre-empting referee arithmetic slips).

### MAJOR issues

1. **[§1 lines 153–155 and §5 lines 350–443, "three independent constructions" claim is weaker than stated.]** The three "independent" 71-rung constructions are: (a) HARM count on TSML's 9×9 sub-magma; (b) cell-disagreement count between TSML and BHML; (c) the prime 71 in disc($f$) for $f \in \Z[x]$ identified with a runtime attractor in an unpublished companion. Of these, (a) and (b) are NOT independent in the strong sense: both are direct cell-by-cell counts on TSML (one with the 0-row stripped, one with a co-defined BHML — but the value 71 enters in (b) via *the* TSML structure too). Claim (c) is the only genuinely external ingredient, and it is contingent on `[Sanders2026Attractor]` (cited as "in preparation"). The defensive-exposition pass in §1 already retreats from "four" to "three" — the right answer at JCT-A standards is *two* genuinely independent structural ingredients (sub-magma count, and the Galois-prime — IF that companion is in print) plus one inclusion-exclusion (72) and one direct linear-algebra computation (70 = det B_YM = C(8,4)).

2. **[§3 Theorem 73 line 320, "ECHO pairs" enumeration] The ECHO-pair list is NOT the ECHO rule of the J14 companion.** J22's "ECHO pairs" {(1,2),(2,4),(2,9),(3,9),(4,8)} and reverses are simply the named non-HARM, non-VOID, non-diagonal exception cells of the TSML table; only (4,8) and (8,4) satisfy J14's defining condition $a+b \equiv ab \pmod{10}$. The other eight cells are exception-class labels carried over from earlier internal documents. **Either rename them ("TSML exception cells") to avoid collision with J14's ECHO rule, or document the discrepancy explicitly in a footnote.** A JCT-A reviewer reading both papers will notice immediately.

3. **[§5 Theorem 71-galois line 387–419, the LMFDB identifier is misquoted.]** The polynomial discriminant disc($f$) = −40896. The LMFDB identifier `4.2.10224.1` decodes as: degree 4, real-signature index 2, **|field discriminant| = 10224**, isomorphism index 1. The factorizations match: 10224 = 2⁴ · 3² · 71 and 40896 = 2⁶ · 3² · 71, so [O_K : Z[α]]² = 40896/10224 = 4 and the index is 2 — entirely consistent — but the manuscript repeatedly writes "disc(4.2.10224.1) = −40896 = −2⁶·3²·71" (lines 153, 246, 249, 391), conflating the polynomial discriminant with the field discriminant. The prime 71 appears in both, so the punchline survives, but the equality $|disc(4.2.10224.1)| = 40896$ is wrong: the field discriminant is 10224. **Fix uniformly throughout.** This is the kind of slip a JCT-A referee with even a passing algebraic-number-theory background will catch.

4. **[§1 line 252–257, "PROVEN" claim] The triple-coincidence at 71 is claimed PROVEN, but rung (c) depends on `Sanders2026Attractor` (in prep).** Until that paper is in print or at least an arXiv preprint, the Galois-form rung is not independently checkable. Either downgrade to COMPUTED/RHYME with explicit dependency disclosure, or release the attractor companion as an arXiv preprint and cite the arXiv number.

5. **[§3, §6, §7 — the 73, 71, 70 rungs all depend on `Sanders2026CLAxioms` (in prep).]** The TSML and BHML tables are not derived in J22; they are imported from "the CL forcing axioms A1–A9" whose paper is unpublished. A JCT-A reviewer will object: a "theorem" of the form "HARM(T) = 73" where T is given by fiat is not a JCT-A theorem, it is a direct count. **You need to either (a) include the table definitions and a short proof that the axioms force them, or (b) cite a published axiom paper, or (c) recast the paper as "Given T and B as in Figure 1, ..." with figures.** The third option is the easiest fix.

6. **[§7 Remark 70-reading line 468–480, Yang–Mills core] The connection "the determinant matching $\binom{8}{4}$ is the integer signature of that bridge" is asserted, not argued.** $\binom{8}{4} = 70$ is the number of 4-element subsets of an 8-element set; the link to the dimension of self-dual / anti-self-dual 4-forms on $\R^8$ is real but standard, and the link to "the Yang–Mills bridge core" is in an unpublished companion (`Sanders2026YangMills`). This rung is a structural rhyme at best.

### MINOR issues

1. **[§1 line 209–215, rung-B-galois claim is stated three times across the paper with slightly different phrasing.]** Lines 153, 391, 422, 425 all assert essentially the same fact. Consolidate.

2. **[§9 lstlisting line 615–620, lens-disagreement snippet imports `canonical_tables`].** Verification scripts in the bundle inline T and B directly (`tsml_bhml_disagreement.py`); the manuscript snippet uses `from canonical_tables import T, B`. Either ship a `canonical_tables.py` in the bundle or inline the matrices in the listing (the wrapper script does).

3. **[§5 line 350–354, three "structural roles"]** "Same integer 71 in three independent constructions" is the load-bearing structural claim. A clean way to make it: state the three constructions as projections of a single algebraic object (the substrate equipped with both tables and the attractor field), and ask whether such a unifying object exists. This is exactly the OPEN list item line 257; foreground it.

4. **[§8 Companion rungs (28, 36, 44)]** Each is asserted with one sentence; Proposition 8.1's connection to dim so(8) = 28 and Prop 8.3's "28+11+5=44" are not derived in the paper. Either prove them or remove the companion section — at JCT-A "two structural roles" is not a publishable claim without verification.

5. **[Title] "Three independent algebraic constructions AND one corollary" reads as four, then specifies in §1 that the "and one corollary" is a step-down.** Consider "Three Algebraic Constructions of an Integer Ladder over Z/10Z" and let the corollary live in §4 without title billing.

### EDITORIAL

1. **[Lens-and-substrate §1 lines 225–239]** The frank disclosure that the tables are "not derived from first principles in this paper" is appreciated by referees; keep it as written.
2. **[§7 line 553–555]** The Monte Carlo Z=21.3, p<10⁻⁵⁰ result cites `Sanders2026CLAxioms` (in prep). Promote this to its own arXiv release before submission; the significance number is the main quantitative defense against the "small-integer coincidence" reading.
3. **[Author affiliations lines 79–86]** Two `\author` blocks with the same author name and different addresses look duplicated — clean up.
4. **[Bibliography line 660–662]** Confirm the Drápal–Wanless citation: the manuscript cites JCTA **184** (2021) 105510 with title "Maximally non-associative quasigroups". J14 cites JCTA **181** (2021) 105444 with title "Maximally nonassociative quasigroups from finite fields". These are two papers; verify and cite both if both are used.
5. **[Throughout, "Tier-B forced" framing]** This is internal-project language ("Tier B" tier-discipline). Replace with conventional referee-facing language: "computed from a fixed canonical table" or "verified by direct enumeration on the construction."

### Journal-fit (JCT-A specific)

JCT-A publishes *structural* combinatorics — enumeration with mathematical depth, identities with combinatorial-grade rigorous proofs. The honest scope of J22 is: a ladder of four small integers appearing on a hand-constructed 10×10 table, three of them HARM-cell counts (one direct, two reductions of the first), one a determinant, plus a number-theoretic coincidence. **The strongest single ingredient is the discriminant fact disc($f$) = −2⁶·3²·71** — but $f$ is defined externally and the connection to T, B is in an unpublished companion. **A JCT-A referee will ask: what is the structural theorem about T, B that produces 71 intrinsically?** Until that question is answered, J22 reads as an integer-coincidence catalog. The natural targets for this material are *Experimental Mathematics* or *Integers* (which publishes integer-pattern catalogs of this flavor) — not JCT-A. **Recommend redirecting to Experimental Mathematics** unless the companion-paper dependencies can be cleared before submission.
