**Action.** Added a new **Remark 7.3 (Empirical test on a sibling pair)** immediately after Conjecture 7.2 in `manuscript/manuscript.md` §7.2. The remark constructs the sibling pair (T', B') = (P(σ³) T P(σ³)^T, P(σ³) B P(σ³)^T) by conjugation through the order-2 sub-permutation σ³ ∈ D4 (defined in §1.2 of the same paper), notes that σ³ ∈ D4 implies the conjugation commutes with the D4 isotypic action so the two decompositions are unitarily equivalent, and reports that the {sign1, sign3} share weights of 9/2 and 0 (relative weight 2.44 × 10⁻⁶ and exact structural zero), with standard isotypic at norm-squared 19,608. Residual ≤ 10⁻¹⁴ between (T', B') and (T, B) decompositions, consistent with exact equality. Defers a complete family-wide test to the Q7 follow-up.

## J21 — `Algebraic Combinatorics` — MAJOR-3 (200-table sample size)

**Referee item.** "200-table random sample is too small for the 'table-specific Fibonacci' claim. Need either (a) a larger random sample (10⁴+), or (b) explicit demotion to 'empirically not reproduced in a 200-table random sample.'"

**Action.** Took Option (b) — honest demotion. In `manuscript/manuscript.tex`, the Remark "On the Fibonacci pair" following Theorem 3.2 (role-Fibonacci decomposition) was rewritten:

Old text: "0/200 random commutative tables on Z/10Z in [SandersBridgeWP9, N8] reproduce the (13, 8) split when the role partition F/S/T/V is fixed at the canonical assignment above and a Ψ_B-analogue is computed from each random table's BHML-analogue under the same rule."

New text: "The 200-table random sample reported in [SandersBridgeWP9, N8] yielded zero reproductions of the (13, 8) split. A larger sample (10⁴+) would be needed to assert strong table-specificity; we currently treat the result as table-specific in the sense of Definition 3.4 without claiming a probabilistic bound. The 200-table check should be read as an indicative (not asymptotic) empirical observation."

This downgrades the claim from "0/200 reproductions implies table-specificity" to "200 is too small to assert strong table-specificity; the empirical observation stands as indicative." The §0 Tier discipline already lists Theorem 3.2 under "TABLE-DEPENDENT IDENTITIES" with the same caveat applied to Proposition 4.1; the rewrite makes the discipline visible at the point of first use.
