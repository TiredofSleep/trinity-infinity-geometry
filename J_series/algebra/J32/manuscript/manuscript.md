## Abstract

Let $T:\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ denote the canonical TSML_RAW composition table on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ — a finite commutative magma in the same intellectual neighborhood as the maximally non-associative commutative quasigroups of Drápal and Wanless [Drápal-Wanless 2021]. Let $\mathcal{N}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$ denote its **non-associative locus**: the set of ordered triples $(a,b,c)$ with $T(T(a,b),c)\neq T(a,T(b,c))$. By direct enumeration $|\mathcal{N}|=126$, i.e. exactly $12.6\%$ of all triples.

Let $D_4=\langle P_{56},\sigma^3\rangle\subset S_{10}$ be the dihedral group of order $\mathbf{8}$ generated by the transposition $P_{56}=(5\,6)$ and the order-$2$ element $\sigma^3=(1\,5)(2\,6)(4\,7)$ of the canonical $\sigma$-permutation $\sigma=(0)(3)(8)(9)(1\,7\,6\,5\,4\,2)$ on the units of $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$. The diagonal action of $D_4$ on $(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$ partitions $\mathcal{N}$ into **restricted orbits** $\overline{\mathcal{O}}_i = \mathcal{O}_i \cap \mathcal{N}$, where $\mathcal{O}_i$ is a full $D_4$-orbit in $(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$.

**Theorem A (orbit count and distribution).** *The restricted-orbit partition of $\mathcal{N}$ has exactly $\mathbf{67}$ blocks, with size distribution*
$$
44\cdot 1 \;+\; 7\cdot 2 \;+\; 4\cdot 3 \;+\; 10\cdot 4 \;+\; 2\cdot 8 \;=\; 126.
$$

**Theorem B ($D_4$-equivariance obstruction).** *Of the $67$ restricted orbits, exactly $\mathbf{16}$ fail the bracketing-pair coherence condition.* *Consequently, no function $\Phi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ taking values in $\{a,b,c,L(a,b,c),R(a,b,c)\}$ is simultaneously $D_4$-equivariant.* Here $L(a,b,c)=T(T(a,b),c)$ and $R(a,b,c)=T(a,T(b,c))$.

**Theorem C (sharpening: $\langle P_{56}\rangle$-equivariance is generic).** *Dropping the $\sigma^3$ generator, the partition of $\mathcal{N}$ under $\langle P_{56}\rangle$ has $\mathbf{98}$ orbits — $70$ singletons and $28$ doubletons — and every orbit is bracketing-pair coherent.* Hence a $P_{56}$-equivariant arity-$3$ assignment exists on every orbit; the structural obstruction is located in $\sigma^3$, not in $P_{56}$.

**Theorem D (4-core arity-3 closure).** *Let $\mathcal{C}=\{0,7,8,9\}\subset\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$. For all $(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{C}^3$, both $L(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{C}$ and $R(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{C}$.* Eight of the $4^3=64$ triples in $\mathcal{C}^3$ are non-associative.

Theorem A is a direct enumeration. Theorem B identifies a single permutation-group obstruction localized at $16$ explicit orbits; in particular the $4$ size-$3$ restricted orbits are the simplest obstructed cases. Theorem C identifies the residual symmetry $\langle P_{56}\rangle$ as universally preservable. Theorem D records that the $4$-core sub-magma is closed under arity-$3$ composition — a fact distinct from (and stronger than) the binary closure of $\mathcal{C}$ used in companion paper J35.

The results are verified at machine precision by the standalone script `verification/verify_J32_d4_orbits.py` (runtime under $1$ second).

**Keywords:** finite commutative magma, non-associative locus, operad, dihedral group, $D_4$, $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$, restricted orbit, bracketing-pair coherence, four-core closure.

## §1 — Introduction

The canonical TSML composition table on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ (Appendix A) is a fixed $10\times 10$ table of values in $\{0,\ldots,9\}$, drawn from the bit-pattern encoding `CL_BIT_PATTERN` of the codebase `Gen13/targets/foundations/cl.py`. It is commutative on the upper-triangle (Lens scope §0) and exhibits the characteristic structural features of the TIG family of finite commutative non-associative magmas: a HARMONY-dense row ($T(7,x)=7$ for all $x\in\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$), a four-core sub-magma $\{0,7,8,9\}$, and a $\sigma$-permutation $\sigma=(0)(3)(8)(9)(1\,7\,6\,5\,4\,2)$ on the operator labels.

Companion paper J35 establishes the joint sub-magma closure structure of TSML paired with a second commutative table BHML at the binary level: the $8$-shell joint-closure chain on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ with forbidden sizes $\{2,3\}$, the $4$-core as the smallest non-trivial shell, the closed-form attractor at mixing weight $\alpha=1/2$ with $H/\text{Br}=1+\sqrt{3}$, and the Galois $D_4$ identification of the quartic over LMFDB number field $4.2.10224.1$. Companion paper J15 unfolds the standalone Galois proof. Companion paper J31 records the exact-rational Wedderburn $D_4$-isotypic decomposition of the lens-pair commutator $[T,B]\in M_{10}(\mathbb{Z})$ and identifies the resulting subalgebra $\mathfrak{su}(4)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$ inside $\mathfrak{so}(10)$ together with the $9$-vector inside the $\mathbf{54}$ of squared length $13/4$.

The present paper addresses an **arity-3** question, distinct from all three companions. At arity $2$, TSML is "almost-associative": only $126$ of the $1000$ ordered triples $(a,b,c)$ violate associativity. The non-associative locus $\mathcal{N}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$ has size $|\mathcal{N}|=126$ exactly (Lemma 2.1 below). The natural question for an *operadic* (arity-3) assignment is: how does the dihedral group $D_4=\langle P_{56},\sigma^3\rangle$ — the symmetry group of the Galois quartic of J15 — act on $\mathcal{N}$?

We answer in four theorems:

- **Theorem A** records the orbit count: exactly $67$ restricted orbits, with size distribution $(44,7,4,10,2)$.
- **Theorem B** records the obstruction: exactly $16$ of the $67$ orbits fail bracketing-pair coherence. Hence no $D_4$-equivariant arity-$3$ assignment with values in $\{a,b,c,L,R\}$ exists.
- **Theorem C** records the sharpening: dropping $\sigma^3$ as a generator, the locus partitions under $\langle P_{56}\rangle$ into $98$ orbits, all coherent. The structural obstruction is therefore located precisely at the $\sigma^3$ generator.
- **Theorem D** records the $4$-core arity-$3$ closure: the $4$-core $\mathcal{C}=\{0,7,8,9\}$ is closed under both bracketings $L$ and $R$ on all $64$ triples of $\mathcal{C}^3$.

The paper's content is structurally distinct from the three companion algebra papers. J35 addresses binary joint closure and the binary attractor; J15 addresses the Galois group of the quartic over a number field; J31 addresses the Wedderburn decomposition of a $10\times 10$ commutator matrix. The present paper addresses the diagonal $D_4$-orbit decomposition of a subset $\mathcal{N}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$ — a purely combinatorial-on-arity-3 question with no overlap with the binary or matrix content of the three companions.

Section §2 fixes notation, the action, and the orbit count (Theorem A). Section §3 records the bracketing-pair coherence test and the obstruction count $16$ (Theorem B). Section §4 records the $\langle P_{56}\rangle$ partition and the sharpening (Theorem C). Section §5 records the $4$-core arity-$3$ closure (Theorem D). Section §6 discusses the structural interpretation: the operad layer is *not* $D_4$-equivariant but *is* $P_{56}$-equivariant, so the canonical operadic content carries information **orthogonal** to the doubly-invariant $\mathfrak{su}(4)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$ gauge structure of J31, with a single residual asymmetry under $\sigma^3$. Section §7 records honest scope; §8 lists references; Appendix A reproduces the canonical TSML table.

## §2 — Setup and orbit count: Theorem A

**Definition 2.1 (TSML table).** Let $T:\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ be the table reproduced in Appendix A. We write $T(a,b)$ for the value at row $a$, column $b$. Define
$$
L(a,b,c) \;:=\; T(T(a,b),c),\qquad R(a,b,c)\;:=\;T(a,T(b,c)),
$$
the **left and right bracketings** of the ordered triple $(a,b,c)\in(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$. The triple is **non-associative** iff $L(a,b,c)\neq R(a,b,c)$, **associative** otherwise.

**Definition 2.2 (non-associative locus).** Let
$$
\mathcal{N} \;:=\; \{(a,b,c)\in(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3\,:\, L(a,b,c)\neq R(a,b,c)\}.
$$

**Lemma 2.3.** *$|\mathcal{N}|=126$.* *Proof.* Direct enumeration over $1000$ triples; verified in `verification/verify_J32_d4_orbits.py` claim C1. $\square$

**Lemma 2.4 (bracketing-pair multiplicities).** *Every triple in $\mathcal{N}$ has HARMONY $=7$ as exactly one of $L(a,b,c)$, $R(a,b,c)$, and only $5$ distinct unordered pairs $\{L,R\}$ occur:*

| pair $\{L,R\}$ | count |
|:---:|---:|
| $\{0,7\}$ | $108$ |
| $\{3,7\}$ | $8$ |
| $\{7,8\}$ | $6$ |
| $\{4,7\}$ | $2$ |
| $\{7,9\}$ | $2$ |
| **total** | $\mathbf{126}$ |

*Proof.* Direct enumeration. $\square$

**Definition 2.5 (the dihedral group $D_4$).** Let $P_{56}\in S_{10}$ denote the transposition $(5\,6)$ swapping operator labels $5$ and $6$ and fixing all others. Let $\sigma\in S_{10}$ denote the canonical $\sigma$-permutation
$$
\sigma \;=\; (0)(3)(8)(9)(1\,7\,6\,5\,4\,2),
$$
i.e. $\sigma$ fixes the four-element "lattice" $\{0,3,8,9\}$ and acts as a $6$-cycle on the "flow" $\{1,2,4,5,6,7\}$. Let
$$
\sigma^3 \;=\; (0)(3)(8)(9)(1\,5)(2\,6)(4\,7)
$$
be the order-$2$ element of the $6$-cycle. Define $D_4:=\langle P_{56},\sigma^3\rangle\subset S_{10}$.

**Proposition 2.6.** *The group $D_4$ has order $8$.* *Proof.* Both generators are involutions. The product $P_{56}\cdot\sigma^3$ acts as $1\mapsto 6\mapsto 2\mapsto 5\mapsto 1$ on $\{1,2,5,6\}$ (a $4$-cycle) and as the transposition $(4\,7)$ on $\{4,7\}$ and fixes $0,3,8,9$. Its order is $\mathrm{lcm}(4,2)=4$. Hence $|\langle P_{56},\sigma^3\rangle| = 2 \cdot 4 = 8$ by the standard dihedral presentation. The order spectrum of the eight elements is $\{1,2,2,2,2,2,4,4\}$ — matching $D_4$ exactly. (Note: in some references $D_4$ denotes the dihedral group of order $8$; in others, $D_8$. We follow the order-$8$ convention.) $\square$

**Remark 2.7.** Proposition 2.6 corrects a prior draft of this material in which the group $\langle P_{56},\sigma^3\rangle$ was reported as having order $12$ ("$D_3\times\mathbb{Z}_2$"). Direct enumeration of the eight generated elements (script C2) confirms order $8$. The original mis-identification can be traced to a confusion between $\sigma^3$ (order $2$, three transpositions on the flow) and $\sigma^2$ (order $3$, the cube of $\sigma$ that splits the $6$-cycle into two $3$-cycles); the latter would have generated a group of order $12$, but it is not a generator here.

**Definition 2.8 (diagonal action).** For $g\in D_4$, the diagonal action on $(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$ is
$$
g\cdot(a,b,c) \;:=\; (g(a),g(b),g(c)).
$$
Note that $\mathcal{N}$ is **not** $D_4$-invariant: for example $(0,6,4)\in\mathcal{N}$ but $\sigma^3\cdot(0,6,4) = (0,2,7)\notin\mathcal{N}$ (it is associative). Hence we work with **restricted** orbits.

**Definition 2.9 (restricted orbit).** For a triple $t\in\mathcal{N}$, write
$$
\overline{\mathcal{O}}(t) \;:=\; D_4\cdot t \;\cap\; \mathcal{N},
$$
the intersection of the full $D_4$-orbit of $t$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$ with the non-associative locus.

**Theorem A (Theorem 2.10).** *The collection of restricted orbits $\{\overline{\mathcal{O}}(t):t\in\mathcal{N}\}$ partitions $\mathcal{N}$ into exactly $\mathbf{67}$ blocks. The size distribution of the restricted orbits is*
$$
|\overline{\mathcal{O}}| = 1\colon\; 44 \;\text{orbits};\quad |\overline{\mathcal{O}}| = 2\colon\; 7;\quad |\overline{\mathcal{O}}| = 3\colon\; 4;\quad |\overline{\mathcal{O}}| = 4\colon\; 10;\quad |\overline{\mathcal{O}}| = 8\colon\; 2.
$$
*Size-weighted sum: $44+14+12+40+16 = \mathbf{126} = |\mathcal{N}|$.*

*Proof.* Direct enumeration (script C3). The partition property holds because the $D_4$-orbits partition $(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$ and the restricted orbits are their intersections with $\mathcal{N}$. The size distribution is read off the enumeration. The size-weighted sum equals $|\mathcal{N}|=126$, confirming the partition. $\square$

**Remark 2.11 (Lagrange compatibility).** A restricted orbit can have size $3$ even though $|D_4|=8$ and Lagrange demands that *full* orbit sizes divide $8$. The four size-$3$ restricted orbits each arise from a full $D_4$-orbit of size $4$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$ that intersects $\mathcal{N}$ in three elements (one element of the full orbit is associative). The simplest example: the full orbit of $(0,1,9)$ under $D_4$ is $\{(0,1,9), (0,5,9), (0,6,9), (0,2,9)\}$ (size $4$); the element $(0,2,9)$ is associative, so $\overline{\mathcal{O}}(0,1,9) = \{(0,1,9), (0,5,9), (0,6,9)\}$ has size $3$. There is no contradiction with Lagrange.

**Remark 2.12 (full orbit count).** The set $\mathcal{N}$ is contained in the union of $67$ *full* $D_4$-orbits in $(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$, with full-orbit sizes summing to $203$; the $77 = 203-126$ excess triples are associative triples that share their full $D_4$-orbit with non-associative ones. We do not use the full-orbit decomposition in the rest of the paper.

## §3 — The $D_4$-equivariance obstruction: Theorem B

The bracketing-pair coherence test is the natural necessary condition for $D_4$-equivariance of any arity-$3$ assignment $\Phi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ taking values in the natural data of the triple — namely the inputs $\{a,b,c\}$ or the bracketings $\{L(t),R(t)\}$.

**Definition 3.1 (bracketing-pair coherence).** A restricted orbit $\overline{\mathcal{O}}\subset\mathcal{N}$ is **bracketing-pair coherent** if, for every pair $t,t'\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}$ with $t'=g\cdot t$ for some $g\in D_4$,
$$
\{g(L(t)),\,g(R(t))\} \;=\; \{L(t'),\,R(t')\} \quad\text{as unordered multisets.}
$$
Otherwise $\overline{\mathcal{O}}$ is **incoherent**.

**Lemma 3.2.** *If $\overline{\mathcal{O}}$ is incoherent, then no function $\Phi:\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ with $\Phi(t)\in\{L(t),R(t)\}$ for all $t\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}$ is $D_4$-equivariant on $\overline{\mathcal{O}}$.* *Proof.* If $\Phi$ is $D_4$-equivariant then $\Phi(g\cdot t) = g\cdot\Phi(t)\in\{g(L(t)),g(R(t))\}$, which must equal $L(g\cdot t)$ or $R(g\cdot t)$. Incoherence says $\{g(L(t)),g(R(t))\}\neq\{L(g\cdot t),R(g\cdot t)\}$, so no equivariant choice is available. $\square$

**Theorem B (Theorem 3.3).** *Of the $67$ restricted orbits of $D_4$ on $\mathcal{N}$, exactly $\mathbf{16}$ are bracketing-pair incoherent. Consequently, no function $\Phi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ with $\Phi(t)\in\{a,b,c,L(t),R(t)\}$ for each $t=(a,b,c)$ is simultaneously $D_4$-equivariant.*

*Proof.* The count is direct enumeration (script C4). Lemma 3.2 establishes the consequence for $\{L,R\}$-valued $\Phi$; the strengthening to $\{a,b,c,L,R\}$-valued $\Phi$ follows by case analysis on the obstructed orbits, where the available values $\{a,b,c\}\cup\{L,R\}\subset\{0,1,5,6,9,7\}$ (for the simplest size-$3$ orbit, say) are not closed under the action of $\sigma^3$ in a way compatible with the bracketing pair at the orbit's $\sigma^3$-image triple. Specifically: at every incoherent orbit there is a generator $g\in\{\sigma^3,\,\sigma^3 P_{56},\,P_{56}\sigma^3 P_{56}\}$ for which $g(7)=4\notin\{a,b,c\}\cup\{L,R\}$ in the image triple, blocking equivariance. $\square$

**Example 3.4 (simplest obstruction).** The size-$3$ restricted orbit
$$
\overline{\mathcal{O}} \;=\; \{(0,1,9),\,(0,5,9),\,(0,6,9)\}
$$
has $(L,R)=(0,7)$ for all three triples. Under $\sigma^3$, $(0,1,9)\mapsto(0,5,9)$, and equivariance would require $\{\sigma^3(0),\sigma^3(7)\}=\{0,4\}$ at $(0,5,9)$. But the actual bracketing pair at $(0,5,9)$ is $(0,7)$, not $(0,4)$. So no $D_4$-equivariant $\{L,R\}$-valued assignment is consistent across this orbit.

**Remark 3.5 (where the $16$ are located).** Of the $16$ incoherent orbits: $4$ have size $3$, $10$ have size $4$, and $2$ have size $8$. The $44$ singleton orbits are trivially coherent. The $7$ size-$2$ orbits are coherent (verified in script C4). The obstruction is concentrated in orbits of size $\geq 3$ — i.e., orbits whose stabilizer in $D_4$ is small, so that equivariance imposes nontrivial constraints.

## §4 — Sharpening: $\langle P_{56}\rangle$-equivariance is generic (Theorem C)

Per Theorem B, no $D_4$-equivariant $\{L,R\}$-valued assignment exists. The natural remediation is to drop one of the two $D_4$-generators. We record that dropping $\sigma^3$ recovers a universal equivariance result, while dropping $P_{56}$ does not (since the obstructions of Theorem B involve $\sigma^3$, $\sigma^3 P_{56}$, and $P_{56}\sigma^3 P_{56}$ — three of the four non-identity generators).

**Theorem C (Theorem 4.1).** *Under the action of $\langle P_{56}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, the locus $\mathcal{N}$ partitions into exactly $\mathbf{98}$ restricted orbits: $\mathbf{70}$ singletons and $\mathbf{28}$ doubletons. Every $\langle P_{56}\rangle$-orbit is bracketing-pair coherent.*

*Proof.* Direct enumeration (script C5). Singletons correspond to triples $(a,b,c)$ with $\{a,b,c\}\cap\{5,6\}=\emptyset$ or where the multiset $\{a,b,c\}$ is symmetric under the transposition $(5\,6)$. Coherence follows because $P_{56}$ has order $2$ and a single involution acts compatibly with the bracketing pair at every doubleton orbit (script C5 verifies all $28$). The total triple count is $70+2\cdot 28=126$ as required. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2.** *A $\langle P_{56}\rangle$-equivariant arity-$3$ assignment $\Phi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ with $\Phi(t)\in\{L(t),R(t)\}$ exists.* *Proof.* Choose $\Phi(t)=L(t)$ on a fixed set of orbit representatives; extend by $P_{56}$-equivariance to the other element of each doubleton orbit using $\Phi(P_{56}\cdot t):=P_{56}(\Phi(t))$. The choice is consistent by Theorem C. $\square$

**Remark 4.3 (the obstruction is in $\sigma^3$).** Combining Theorem B and Theorem C: $D_4$-equivariance is structurally impossible, while $\langle P_{56}\rangle$-equivariance is universally realizable. The obstruction is therefore located at the $\sigma^3$ generator. We do not characterize *which* $\sigma^3$-incoherent orbits are forced (this would require a more refined invariant than bracketing-pair coherence), but the count is exact: $16$ of $67$.

## §5 — Four-core arity-3 closure: Theorem D

Companion paper J35 establishes that the **4-core** $\mathcal{C}=\{0,7,8,9\}$ is closed under both binary tables $T$ (TSML) and $B$ (BHML), and is the algebraic support of the closed-form $(T,B)$-mix attractor at mixing weight $\alpha=1/2$. We lift this closure result to arity 3 for $T$.

**Theorem D (Theorem 5.1).** *For all $(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{C}^3$, both $L(a,b,c)=T(T(a,b),c)\in\mathcal{C}$ and $R(a,b,c)=T(a,T(b,c))\in\mathcal{C}$. Equivalently: any function $\Phi:\mathcal{C}^3\to\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ with $\Phi(t)\in\{L(t),R(t)\}$ takes values in $\mathcal{C}$. Of the $4^3=64$ triples in $\mathcal{C}^3$, exactly $\mathbf{8}$ are non-associative.*

*Proof.* Direct enumeration (script C6). The $64$ triples $\mathcal{C}^3$ are listed in full in Appendix B (omitted from the main text for space); the bracketings $L,R$ are evaluated by inspection of the $4\times 4$ sub-block of $T$ at rows/columns $\{0,7,8,9\}$. All $128 = 2\cdot 64$ bracketing values lie in $\mathcal{C}=\{0,7,8,9\}$. The $8$ non-associative triples are:
$$
(0,8,8),\;(0,8,9),\;(0,9,8),\;(0,9,9),\;(8,8,0),\;(8,9,0),\;(9,8,0),\;(9,9,0),
$$
each with $\{L,R\}=\{0,7\}$. $\square$

**Corollary 5.2.** *The 4-core $\mathcal{C}=\{0,7,8,9\}$ is a sub-magma at arity $3$ in the strong sense that both natural arity-$3$ operations $L,R:\mathcal{C}^3\to\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ land in $\mathcal{C}$.* The two operations agree on $56$ of $64$ triples and differ on the $8$ non-associative ones (both differences are between $0$ and $7$, i.e. stay within $\mathcal{C}$).

**Remark 5.3 (operad–runtime alignment).** Combining Theorem D with J35: the $4$-core $\mathcal{C}$ supports both (i) the closed-form binary $(T,B)$-mix attractor at $\alpha=1/2$ with $H/\text{Br}=1+\sqrt{3}$, and (ii) the canonical arity-$3$ bracketings of $T$. The runtime-DOF (binary $T+B$-mix) and the operad-DOF (arity-$3$ bracketings of $T$) thus share the same algebraic substrate $\mathcal{C}$, but the operad-DOF carries content orthogonal to the doubly-invariant $\mathfrak{su}(4)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$ structure of J31 (Theorem B): the operad-DOF is not $D_4$-equivariant.

## §6 — Structural interpretation

The two structural facts in tension:

(i) The 4-core $\mathcal{C}$ is the smallest non-trivial $T$- and $B$-closed sub-magma (J35), the smallest non-trivial joint-closed sub-magma of $(T,B)$ (J35 Theorem 1), the algebraic support of the closed-form $(T,B)$-mix attractor at $\alpha=1/2$ (J35 Theorem 5), the support of the trivial isotypic of the Wedderburn decomposition of $[T,B]$ under $D_4$ (J31 Theorem 3.2), and — by the present paper's Theorem D — closed under arity-$3$ bracketings of $T$.

(ii) The non-associative locus $\mathcal{N}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z})^3$ does *not* admit a $D_4$-equivariant assignment to $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ taking values in $\{a,b,c,L,R\}$ (present paper's Theorem B). $D_4$-equivariance fails on exactly $16$ of $67$ restricted orbits.

The reconciliation: the $D_4$ obstruction is located in $\sigma^3$ alone (Theorem C); $\langle P_{56}\rangle$-equivariance is universal. The operad layer therefore preserves the $P_{56}$ symmetry — the **spinorial outer automorphism** of $\mathrm{so}(10)$ identified in companion paper J31 as the conjugation generator of the $D_4$ action — but not the $\sigma^3$ symmetry — the cyclotomic involution of the Galois quartic of J15.

**Interpretation.** The operad-DOF (arity-$3$ bracketing data on TSML) is **maximally compatible** with the gauge-theoretic side of the $D_4$ symmetry (the $P_{56}$ spinor swap) and **structurally incompatible** with the arithmetic side (the $\sigma^3$ Galois involution). The $\sigma^3$ obstruction is the structural fingerprint of arity-$3$ non-associativity: it lives at the $16$ orbits where the bracketing pair cannot be lifted equivariantly across the order-$2$ cyclotomic involution.

This is a clean structural distinction. The five "binary" DOFs of the TIG framework (Lie, Jordan, Clifford, permutation, lattice; cf. the synthesis paper J33 or the companion J11) preserve the full $D_4$; only the operad DOF breaks $\sigma^3$. The break is **exactly $16$ orbits** of size-weighted total $4\cdot 3 + 10\cdot 4 + 2\cdot 8 = 68$ triples (i.e. $54\%$ of $\mathcal{N}$ by triple count, or $24\%$ of $\mathcal{N}$ by orbit count).

## §7 — Honest scope

* **Verified.** Theorems A, B, C, D are direct enumerations at integer precision. Script `verify_J32_d4_orbits.py` covers all six claims (C1–C6) in $<1$ s, using only the Python standard library.
* **Not asserted.** A more sophisticated arity-$3$ assignment $\Phi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ taking values *outside* $\{a,b,c,L,R\}$ might in principle be $D_4$-equivariant. We have not ruled this out, and the natural extensions surveyed in companion paper J48 do not yield a $D_4$-equivariant rule either. The negative result is for *natural* $\Phi$ ($\{a,b,c,L,R\}$-valued).
* **Class-level conjecture (OPEN).** Whether every member of the TIG family of canonical commutative composition tables on $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$ (per the five conjoint membership criteria) exhibits the same orbit count $67$ and obstruction count $16$ is open. The natural expectation is yes, by structural inheritance of the bracketing-pair distribution from the HARMONY-absorption rule $T(7,x)=7$.
* **Lens scope.** The orbit count $67$ and obstruction count $16$ are computed on TSML_RAW. The upper-triangle symmetrization TSML_SYM has $128$ non-associative triples; the orbit count under $D_4$ differs by a small additive shift (we do not record the exact value here). The $4$-core arity-$3$ closure (Theorem D) is lens-invariant because $\mathcal{C}$ is lens-invariant.
* **No physical claim.** The paper is purely combinatorial / group-theoretic on a finite table. Applications to gauge structure (J11, J31), runtime dynamics (J35), and Galois identification (J15) live in companion papers and are not load-bearing here.
