## 1. What actually winds

Work in ℤ/10ℤ under **multiplication mod 10** (the TSML substrate; the
`AG(2,3)` operator labeling assigns the ten residues to the ten σ-operators, but
the winding claim needs only the arithmetic). Partition the nonzero residues:

| set | residues | operators | multiplicative role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **corners** | {1, 3, 7, 9} | LATTICE, PROGRESS, HARMONY, COLLAPSE | the unit group U(10) = (ℤ/10ℤ)ˣ |
| **edges** | {2, 4, 6, 8} | COUNTER, ?, CHAOS, BREATH | the "even non-zero" coset |
| **center** | {5} | BALANCE | CRT idempotent (1,0) |
| **void** | {0} | RESET/VOID | absorbing |

Two closed cycles live here, and **these are the objects that wind**:

- **Corner cycle** `1 → 3 → 9 → 7 → 1`, generated by repeatedly ×3.
  (Powers of 3 mod 10: 3⁰=1, 3¹=3, 3²=9, 3³=7, 3⁴=1.)
- **Edge cycle** `6 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 6`, generated by repeatedly ×2, with **6** as
  the (multiplicative) identity of that set.

Each is a cyclic group of order 4 (≅ ℤ/4ℤ). Both are, in fact, copies of
(ℤ/5ℤ)ˣ: reduce mod 5 and the corners map {1,3,9,7}→{1,3,4,2} and the edges map
{6,2,4,8}→{1,2,4,3}, each a bijection onto (ℤ/5ℤ)ˣ = {1,2,3,4}.

**The parameter / loop.** The "loop" is simply one full traversal of a 4-cycle by
its generator: start at the identity, multiply by the generator four times, and
you return to the start. There is no continuous parameter and no Hamiltonian —
the loop is a discrete orbit of length 4 in a finite group. This is the first
honest downgrade from the physics vocabulary: "loop" here means "orbit of a
cyclic group," nothing more.

## 2. The winding number, with the computation

Give each element of the order-4 group a **phase** by its discrete logarithm.
Fix a primitive root of (ℤ/5ℤ)ˣ, say 2 (so 2⁰=1, 2¹=2, 2²=4, 2³=3), and set

  θ(x) = 2π · dlog₂(x mod 5) / 4  ∈ {0, π/2, π, 3π/2}.

This embeds each cycle into the unit circle S¹ as the four 4th-roots of unity.
The **winding number** of a cycle is the total principal-branch argument
increment divided by 2π:

  W = (1 / 2π) · Σ_steps  arg( e^{iθ(x_{next})} / e^{iθ(x_{cur})} ),

with each `arg` taken in (−π, π] so the *direction* of each step is detected.

Carrying it out (verified numerically in `verify_berry_winding.py`):

- **Edge cycle** `6→2→4→8`: mod-5 images 1,2,4,3 → dlog₂ = 0,1,2,3 → each step
  **+π/2** → total **+2π** → **W_edge = +1**.
- **Corner cycle** `1→3→9→7`: mod-5 images 1,3,4,2 → dlog₂ = 0,3,2,1 → each step
  **−π/2** → total **−2π** → **W_corner = −1**.

So the ordered pair of winding numbers is **(W_corner, W_edge) = (−1, +1)**,
i.e. the two cycles circle the same S¹ in **opposite directions**. This is the
"`(+1,−1)`" of the Two-Cross Theorem (the overall labeling sign is a convention;
see §3).

**Where the content really is.** For a *generator* of a cyclic group of order 4,
winding magnitude 1 is automatic — a generator by definition makes exactly one
circuit in 4 steps. So `|W| = 1` carries no information. The **non-trivial,
basis-independent invariant is the relative sign**:

  W_corner · W_edge = −1   ("counter-rotation" / opposite chirality).

And that sign has a clean torus-free cause: **the two generators are mutual
inverses mod 5**,

  3 · 2 = 6 ≡ 1  (mod 5),   i.e.  3 ≡ 2⁻¹ (mod 5).

Advancing the corners by 3 is stepping by −1 in the exponent of the shared ℤ/4ℤ
exactly when advancing the edges by 2 is stepping by +1. That is the entire
honest algebraic content of "one winds +1, the other winds −1." It needs no
surface, no torus, no π₁.
