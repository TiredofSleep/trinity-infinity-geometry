## 1. The argument [NAMED: Livné 1995; Serre; Hecke]

Let n ∈ {7, 11, 15}, and ρ = ρ_n (for n = 15, ρ = ρ_15 ⊗ (5|·)).
1. **ρ is two-dimensional and orthogonal, with distinct Hodge–Tate weights.**
   - dim V_n = ⌈n/6⌉ − [3 | n] = 2.
   - The cup product on the middle cohomology, of even degree n − 3, is symmetric and nondegenerate on the
     sign-isotypic part.
   - Since n ≡ 3 (mod 4), there is no middle Hodge class, so the two Hodge types are a conjugate pair (p, q), (q, p)
     with p ≠ q.
2. **det ρ = (D|·), with D = −35, −1155, −3003**, by Theorem 8 ([`DETERMINANTS.md`](DETERMINANTS.md)). Twisting by
   (5|·) does not change a two-dimensional determinant.
3. **ρ is induced** (Livné's argument).
   - A reducible orthogonal ρ with nontrivial determinant splits into two anisotropic lines with quadratic characters.
     Their Hodge–Tate weights would both be 0, so ρ is irreducible.
   - On G_K, K = Q(√D), the determinant is trivial, so ρ(G_K) lies in SO(2), which is abelian. Hence
     ρ ≅ Ind_K^Q ψ.
   - ψ is Hodge–Tate, so it is locally algebraic and comes from an algebraic Hecke character (Tate, Serre), of
     infinity type z² for weight 3.
4. **ψ is unramified.**
   - The conductor of Ind ψ is |D|·N(f_ψ). Corollary 2 gives ρ's conductor: 35 and 1155 at n = 7 and 11.
   - At 15, inertia at 5 acts on V15 by two reflections (two orbits of nodes with odd valuation), that is, by −1 on the
     whole piece. Twisting by (5|·) removes it, leaving 3003 = |D|. So N(f_ψ) = 1.
5. **ψ is one of 2, 8 or 8 characters.**
   - K's class group is (Z/2)^r, with r = 1, 3, 3, and it is generated by the ramified primes 𝔭₅; 𝔭₃, 𝔭₅, 𝔭₇; and
     𝔭₃, 𝔭₇, 𝔭₁₁ respectively. It is checked by reduced forms, and every split prime below 3000 lies in exactly one of
     their classes.
   - Since ψ(𝔭_l)² = ψ((l)) = l², ψ(𝔭_l) = s_l·l. Since ψ((√D)) = D, the product of all the s_l is −1.
6. **The candidates' traces, exactly.**
   - For a split prime p in the class of 𝔞_S = ∏_(l ∈ S) 𝔭_l, there is γ = (u + v√D)/2 with (γ) = 𝔭𝔞_S, so
     u² + |D|v² = 4pm, where m = ∏S.
   - Then a_p = (γ² + γ̄²)/ψ(𝔞_S) = (2pm + Dv²)/(m·∏_(l ∈ S) s_l). At inert primes a_p = 0.
   - By Hecke, Σ ψ(𝔟)q^(N𝔟) is a newform of weight 3, level |D| and character (D|·), and ρ is its Galois
     representation.
7. **Which one** [measured].
   - Of the 2^r candidates, exactly one has the measured traces at every good prime from 11 to 401: 75, 74 and 73
     primes. The traces are exact counts made as in rounds 20 to 24, read as V_n's traces by Theorem 8's Step 1.
   - Its signs agree with the counts at the bad primes, which have only nodes there: a₅ = −5 and a₇ = 7 at n = 7;
     a₅ = 5, a₇ = −7 and a₁₁ = 11 at n = 11; a₇ = 7, a₁₁ = −11 and a₁₃ = 13 at n = 15.
   - They also agree with a₃ = +3, which the L-function's functional equation selected
     ([`verify_conductors.py`](verify_conductors.py)).
   - At n = 7 the candidate's a₂ = 0 and a₃ = 1, which no count of ours reaches, are LMFDB's.

Steps 1 to 6 prove that V11 and V15 ⊗ (5|·) are CM newforms of levels 1155 and 3003. They need the measured traces
nowhere, only Theorem 8 and Corollary 2. Step 7 names which form, and it is the one step that reads the counts.
