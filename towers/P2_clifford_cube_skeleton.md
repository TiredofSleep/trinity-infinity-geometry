# P2 — The Forced Clifford Cube and Its Two Projections
## SKELETON. Companion to P1 (Integers as Geometric Entities).

## Abstract (draft)

Continuing the reading of integer *n* as an *n*-point configuration (P1), we show
that the chain **4 → tetrahedron → cube (8 = 2×4) → three orthogonal axes** produces
the geometric (Clifford) algebra **Cl(3)**, of dimension 2³ = 8, whose eight basis
elements correspond to the cube's eight vertices (two tetrahedra). We prove the two
canonical planar projections of this algebra are the **square** (four-fold, face view)
and the **hexagon** (three-fold, body-diagonal view), and that the angle relating
them is exactly **cos²(1,1,1) = 1/3** — the same 1/3 forced in P1 as 1/(N−1) at N = 4.
The paper's claim is precise: *the integer chain reaches the cube, whose three
perpendicular axes generate Cl(3); the square and the hexagon are its two projections;
the 1/3 is the projection angle.* It is **not** claimed that Clifford structure lives
natively in the hexagonal lattice — the hexagon's directions are 120° apart, not
perpendicular, so they do not anticommute — but that the hexagon is the three-fold
*shadow* of the Clifford cube.

## 1. From integer 4 to the cube [FORCED]

- Recall from P1: 1,2,3,4 are the simplices; 4 = the tetrahedron (3-simplex, first
  solid).
- **The tetrahedron is half the cube.** Its four vertices are four alternating cube
  corners; two opposite-parity tetrahedra compose the cube (*stella octangula*, Kepler
  1619). Hence **8 = 2 × 4**: the cube is the doubled tetrahedron, its two halves
  swapped by the central inversion (each tetrahedron is achiral — its own mirror image).
  [FORCED / NAMED: Coxeter 1973]

## 2. The cube is Cl(3) [FORCED]

- **Clifford algebra Cl(3)** is generated by three vectors e₁,e₂,e₃ satisfying
  eᵢeⱼ + eⱼeᵢ = 2δᵢⱼ (orthonormal, anticommuting) [Clifford 1878; Hestenes 1966].
- **The cube's three orthogonal axes are exactly these generators.** *Verification*
  (represent the axes by Pauli matrices, a faithful rep of the Cl(3) vector space):
  - {e₀,e₁} = {e₀,e₂} = {e₁,e₂} = 0 — **all anticommute** ✓ (computed)
  - e₀² = e₁² = e₂² = I — **each squares to the identity** ✓
- **Dimension.** Cl(3) has 2³ = 8 basis elements, graded **1 + 3 + 3 + 1** (Pascal's
  row 3: scalar, 3 vectors, 3 bivectors, pseudoscalar) — a palindrome, and the 8
  matches the cube's 8 vertices = 2 tetrahedra. [FORCED]
- **The bivectors are rotations — the imaginary unit is intrinsic.** Each grade-2 element
  (a face-plane, e.g. e₁e₂) satisfies **(e₁e₂)² = −1** [FORCED, computed] — the defining
  property of *i*. So Cl(3) carries rotation without importing it: the three bivectors
  {e₁e₂, e₁e₃, e₂e₃} are the three orthogonal rotation-planes of space (the generators of
  Spin(3) ≅ SU(2)), and the pseudoscalar e₁e₂e₃ also squares to −1. The "*i*" is not added
  to the cube; it *is* the cube's face-plane. [FORCED / NAMED: Hestenes 1966]
- **Conclusion of §2:** the integer chain reaches the cube, and the cube's three
  perpendicular axes generate Cl(3). This is the precise sense of "the integers lead to
  Clifford": any three perpendicular directions generate Cl(3); what the chain supplies is
  the cube that carries them.

## 3. The two projections [FORCED]

- **Face projection.** Viewed along a face normal, the three orthogonal axes project
  to a **square** — four-fold symmetry, 90°. [FORCED]
- **Diagonal projection.** Viewed along the body diagonal (1,1,1) — the cube's 3-fold
  rotation axis — the three orthogonal axes project to **three directions 120° apart:
  a regular hexagon**, three-fold. *Verification:* the projected axes are at 120.00°
  (computed). [FORCED]
- **The relating angle is 1/3.** The direction cosine of (1,1,1) with any axis is
  1/√3; **cos² = 1/3** — identical to the P1 value 1/(N−1) at N = 4. So the *same* 1/3
  that fixes the tetrahedral angle also fixes the projection relating the square and
  hexagon. [FORCED]

## 4. What is and is not claimed [READING / boundary]

- **Claimed [FORCED]:** the cube's three perpendicular axes generate Cl(3); the square and
  the hexagon are the cube's two projections; 1/3 is the projection angle.
- **NOT claimed:** that the hexagonal *lattice* carries Clifford structure. Clifford
  generators are directions, and two directions anticommute exactly when they are
  perpendicular (eᵢeⱼ + eⱼeᵢ = 2 eᵢ·eⱼ); the hexagon's directions are 120° apart, so they
  **do not anticommute**. (It is the direction vectors that anticommute — the reflection
  maps in two perpendicular mirrors commute.) The hexagon is a *projection* of the Clifford
  cube, not a host of the algebra. [This is the fence that keeps §2 honest; recorded, not
  hidden.]

## 5. Conclusion (draft)

The integer chain of P1, continued one step, forces the geometric algebra Cl(3): the
cube is the doubled tetrahedron, its three orthogonal axes anticommute and generate
the 8-dimensional algebra, and its two canonical projections are the square (four-fold)
and the hexagon (three-fold), related by the projection angle cos²(1,1,1) = 1/3. This
is the exact and defensible meaning of "the integers lead to Clifford": the algebra is
carried *by the cube*, and the square and the hexagon are its two shadows. The hexagon does not host the algebra; it is
the algebra's three-fold projection, and saying otherwise would overclaim.
