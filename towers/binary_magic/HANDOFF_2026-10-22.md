## Where this came from
Brayden asked for "a full binary matrix assembly for the whole world of possible magic squares built by
the cube's corners" (cube corners 0–7 written in binary; 0 and 7 coincide down the body diagonal).
That became an enumeration of all 4×4 magic squares on 0–15 (the tesseract's corners) read bit by bit.
His later intuitions steered it: "every one is three, then it's all the same again", the trefoil with
petals, crossings and a central crossing, "go Fourier", "fold recipes", and qubit vs qutrit.
Each one became a test with a pass/fail.

## How to run
All scripts are plain Python 3 (numpy; sympy and scipy for some). Run from inside `binary_magic/`:
1. `python3 binary_magic.py` writes `squares_4x4.json` (all 880, Frénicle form). Everything else reads it.
2. Every other script is independent and asserts its own claims. All passed at handoff.
   `split_check.py` takes about 90 seconds; the rest take a few seconds each.

   `gcc -O2 -o /tmp/anneal8 order8/anneal8.c -lm` (same for `anneal8_binary.c`).
   The data files are already there: `squares8_annealed.txt` (2,000) and `squares8_bitbalanced.txt` (30).

**Task 0 (done):** `python3 run_all.py` runs all 20 scripts and prints ALL GREEN (verified 2026-10-22). Keep it green.

## Results by status
Statuses: **known** (published by someone else) · **proved** (hand proof, written down) ·
**exhaustion** (true for all 880 by computation; no reason found) · **enumeration** (counts) ·
**reading** (interpretation, fenced; never a premise).

**Known**
- 880 squares (Frénicle 1693); Dudeney's 12 groups (1917); 48 pandiagonal squares.
- Rows and columns of every square XOR to 0 (Conway–Norton–Ryba 2017, "Nimm0"). With line sum 30,
  this means every bit plane has exactly two 1s per row and column.
- Takemura's classification by degree 240 / 624 / 16 (arXiv:2601.06131, Jan 2026, unrefereed;
  reproduced here).
- 10 two-qubit Mermin–Peres grids; the KCBS pentagram (classical 2, quantum √5).

**Proved** (details in `ATLAS.md`, `FOLD_PROOF.md`, `degree_explained.py`)
- Carry rules on a line of 4: carries ±1, contiguous, same sign; the pattern "1→2 with 4→8" alone is
  impossible (the four candidate lines sum to 20, 24, 36 or 40, never 30).
- Bit plane k is balanced exactly when the carries on both sides of it are unused.
- Takemura degree 3 ⟺ invertible: APD₃ = 24·det(A)/s for any matrix with constant row sum s.
- An even complement rearrangement forces an even degree (so the square is singular); an odd one
  forces an odd degree. The complement fold (b = 1111) is an even rearrangement.
- Degree ∞ mechanism: rows or columns pair up by single Walsh patterns, plus one linear condition.
- **Every square folds** (has a left–right or top–bottom mirror with A(mirror x) = A(x) XOR b),
  given Nimm0. A side result: every bit plane has four 1s among corners + center, so the diagonals
  cancel plane by plane.
- Any XOR symmetry zeroes at least half the full binary spectrum.
- Knot test: on the trefoil projection, the carry signs can only produce the two knotted sign
  patterns. The 48 whole-only squares are the ones that knot.

**Exhaustion only** (true for all 880; prove these)
- Singular ⟺ complement fold; invertible ⟺ 3-bit fold.
- The center always has a complement fold; the petals never have one.
- Nothing besides the Walsh-pairing mechanism gives degree ∞; degree 6 never occurs.

**Enumeration**
- Triad counts: center 576 (432 affine), petals 192, crossings 112.
- 12,631,127,040 affine fully binary magic squares of order 8 (the same method gives 432 at order 4).
- Exactly one 3×3 square is both a magic square (sum 30) and a Mermin–Peres square:
  `11 13 6 / 5 10 15 / 14 7 9` = `XY YZ ZX / ZZ XX YY / YX ZY XZ` (one labeling out of 28).

**Order 8 (sampled, not proved):** 2,000 annealed squares and 30 bit-balanced ones show no folds,
no plane-by-plane diagonal cancellation and no singular squares. At order 4 the fold is a law; at
order 8 it holds only for a family (affine squares and maybe near-affine ones). The proof fails at
step 1: on 8 cells, 14 of the 70 balanced rows are single Walsh patterns.

**Reading (fenced, never a premise)**
- The trefoil picture (cube seen down its diagonal) is an exact layout match, but the knot is an
  encoding of the carry-sign rule, not a property of the squares themselves.
- Dirac/Clifford: the 16 values are the Dirac algebra's 16 elements; lines multiply to ±1. This is a
  faithful re-encoding of XOR, not physics.
- "Measurable residue sits on a locked, balanced structure": supported by the sandbox tests (random
  vs semi-magic vs magic) and by metrology (bridges, LIGO, the SI quantum standards). Analogy, not a
  derivation.
- Metasurfaces: every bit plane, used as a 0/π tile, is silent along both axes (ideal-array model).

## Addendum: results after the first write-up
- **Artifact:** the interactive "Binary Magic Atlas" page is published (the source is
  `artifact/atlas_template.html`; the data comes from `artifact/atlas_data.py`). Rebuild by injecting
  `atlas_data.json` into the template.
- **Two qutrits:** no 3×3 grid of commuting two-qutrit operators with lines multiplying to the identity
  exists (**proved**, checked in `qutrit_grids.py`). The four corners fix the grid. The last row needs
  [x01,x10] = [x00,x11] and the last column needs [x01,x10] = −[x00,x11]. Mod 2 these coincide
  (the Mermin square); mod 3 they force both to be 0, and nine cells don't fit in an isotropic plane
  with eight non-identity points.

- **Novelty check (`NOVELTY.md`):** the 528 / 192 / 112 / 48 split and the row/column balance are
  Fitting's (1931). Probably new: the carry formalism, degree 3 ⟺ invertible, the parity theorem, the
  fold theorem, the 432 and order-8 affine counts, the magic Mermin square and the two-qutrit no-grid
  result. Still unread: Conway–Norton–Ryba (paywalled) and Fitting 1931 (order 8). Replace Task 3
  ("prove Nimm0") with "obtain and cite Fitting 1931".
- **"368,640" verified:** 1,105,920 bent-diagonal Franklin squares, of which 368,640 are pandiagonal
  (Schindel–Rempel–Loly 2006).
