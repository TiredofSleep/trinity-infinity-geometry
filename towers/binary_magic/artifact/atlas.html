How the three lenses line up

Read each square through its bits (which carries its diagonals need), its folds (a mirror that turns every number n into n XOR b), and its signs (determinant and Takemura's degree). The bits choose the region. The folds choose the signs. The crossings are where both kinds of fold meet.

Every mask is a reflection measured from 7.5, the value no cell holds: n XOR b = c − n, with the centre c/2 at 7.5 moved by ±2q−1 for each bit q that b leaves alone. Only the complement, b = 1111, leaves none, so only it reflects through 7.5 itself, and that forces a kernel. Under a left–right complement fold the four columns, weighted 1, −1, −1, 1, add to zero.

What is known and what is proved

Known means published before. F. Fitting (1931), building on an idea of his son Hans, already had the binary layers, the carry rules and the counts. Proved means a written hand proof here. Exhaustion means true for all 880 by computation, with no reason found yet.

The quantum magic square

One 3×3 square is both an ordinary magic square and a Mermin–Peres square, the standard proof that quantum measurements cannot have preset values. It is unique across all 28 ways of labeling two-qubit operators by numbers. It is also the only 3×3 magic square with sum 30 whose rows and columns all XOR to zero.

In base 4 each number is two digits from {1, 2, 3}. Each digit layer is a Latin square, which is how the Lo Shu is built. Rows and columns have all-different digits, so they cancel: 1 XOR 2 XOR 3 = 0. Each diagonal repeats one digit, which leaves a residue, so its operators cannot be measured together.

The rows multiply to −1 and the columns to +1. No assignment of ±1 values satisfies all six lines.

For two qutrits no such grid exists at all. Fix the four corner cells and the rest follows. The last row closes only if two commutators are equal, and the last column only if they are opposite. In binary +1 equals −1, so both close. In base 3 both commutators are forced to zero, and nine cells cannot fit in a plane that holds eight.

Signs at the ends of rows and columns are the products of the operators on that line. Dashed cells are the main diagonal.

At order 8 it becomes a family

Across 2,000 sampled 8×8 magic squares on 0–63, none folds, none has diagonals that cancel bit by bit, and none is singular. Balance alone does not bring the fold back: 17 sampled squares whose six bit layers are balanced on every line have no XOR symmetry at all. The fold survives in structured families. There are exactly 12,631,127,040 affine fully binary 8×8 magic squares, and every one folds. Every square in Fitting's 1931 family has an XOR symmetry too, under a half-shift rather than a mirror, and almost none of those is affine. The 4×4 proof needs every balanced row of four cells to be a pure Walsh pattern. On eight cells only 14 of the 70 balanced rows are.
