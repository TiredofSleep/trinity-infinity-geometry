# Language as measurement — many rulers, one object

**Started 2026-06-13.** A working direction, not a finished claim. The aim
(Brayden): *convert English into shapes based on wave math, and curvature
between words and topics — using each scientific domain (thermodynamics,
electrical engineering, particle physics) as a measurement system.* The deeper
principle: **there is no single correct way to measure information. Any
*consistent* ruler is valid, as long as it produces a layer of unique, valuable
information about the thing being studied.**

This folder is the opposite of the table-fitting work. We are not asking "what
is the one true algebra of reality." We are building a **kit of rulers** and
asking what each one *reads* — and, crucially, using geometry + algebra to see
**when two subjects collapse into one, where domains cross, and where they
overlap.**

## The reframe (what this is *not*)

The earlier "geometry-guided growth" idea was about growing/pruning a neural
network's layers. That was the wrong target. The geometry is a **measurement
system for knowledge itself**:

- **collapse** — two subjects that look distinct are the *same* under a ruler
  (entropy in thermodynamics ≡ Shannon noise in EE ≡ disorder in stat-mech).
- **crossing** — two domains meet at a shared structure (a resonance in EE is a
  standing wave is an eigenmode is a bound state).
- **overlap** — partial shared substructure that is worth naming.

"Grow vs prune," re-read honestly: **collapse the redundant, surface the
crossings.**

## The two rulers (built, computed, honest) — `rulers.py`

Run `python rulers.py`. Both rulers read the **defect**, not the summary.

### Ruler A — a circle measured by squares
Tile the disk with an `n×n` grid. Interior squares give a lower-bound area; the
boundary squares are the defect.

| n | inside | boundary | area (lo) | taxicab (boundary·h) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 164 | 60 | 2.56 | 7.50 |
| 64 | 3080 | 252 | 3.01 | 7.88 |
| 256 | 50920 | 1020 | 3.108 | 7.97 |

The interior **resolves the area** → π (3.108 at n=256, bracket → 0). But the
same squares measure the boundary as **taxicab length → 8r = 8.0**, *never* the
Euclidean 2πr = 6.28 — and refining the grid does **not** fix it (the staircase
paradox, "why π ≠ 4"). **Lesson: the ruler decides which invariant you can read.
Curvature is invisible to squares; to see it you must switch rulers.** This is
the whole thesis in one honest computation.

### Ruler B — a line measured by a parabolic envelope
The Chebyshev staircase ψ(x) = Σ_{p^k ≤ x} log p trends like the line y = x. The
defect ψ(x) − x stays inside a **√x envelope**: empirically |ψ(x) − x| ≤
0.78·√x for x up to 10⁵, and the running max grows with exponent **≈ 0.52** (RH
predicts exactly ½). The straight-line summary throws the information away; the
**residual, and its scaling exponent, is the information** — the exponent *names
the mechanism class* (½ = square-root cancellation; > ½ would mean structure the
line can't hold). Figure: `rulers_figure.svg` (generated by
`make_rulers_figure.py`, straight from these numbers).

These two rulers are **duals**: circle ↔ line, area ↔ length, 2-D square grid ↔
1-D parabolic envelope. Together they are a starter basis for measuring
information.

## The method — domain-as-ruler, recursive meta → letter

For each domain used as a measurement system, decompose the object top-down:

```
meta (the whole)
  → 2 or 3 categories          (the domain's primary dichotomy)
    → operations / parts        (what the domain *does*)
      → finer splits …          (macro → micro)
        → the letter            (the quantum of the medium)
```

At every level, geometry + algebra mark **collapses, crossings, overlaps**.
Worked micro-example — the **thermodynamics** ruler applied to *meaning in a
text*:

```
meaning (a text as an energy system)
├── ENERGY        content / information density
│     ├── heat    affective charge (undirected)
│     └── work    assertion / directed claim
├── ENTROPY       ambiguity / spread of interpretation     ← collapses with
│                                                             EE-ruler "noise"
└── FLOW          how meaning transfers across the boundary  (reader ↔ text)
        ↓ … down to the sentence, the word, the letter (a microstate)
```

Run the **EE** ruler on the same text and its "noise / channel capacity" branch
**collapses onto** thermodynamics' ENTROPY branch — that collapse is a *crossing*
the geometry should detect automatically, not a coincidence we assert.

## English → shapes (the next build)

Each word/topic gets a position in an embedding space; the **curvature** of the
path between consecutive words/topics (how sharply meaning turns) and the **wave**
signature (amplitude / frequency / phase of the drift) are the measured shape.
Then Ruler B applies directly: walking a topic is a "line," its semantic drift
has a √-style envelope, and the **deviations are the information**.

Immediate next step: `curvature.py` — embed a concept chain (local MiniLM,
already available), compute discrete curvature + the envelope of semantic drift,
and feed real numbers into a second figure. Then a `lens.py` that runs the
recursive decomposition for a chosen domain and a `crossings.py` that detects
collapses/crossings across two lenses.

## Honest scope

- **Computed and real:** the two rulers (`rulers.py`), the figure
  (`make_rulers_figure.py`).
- **Designed scaffold, not yet computed:** the domain-lens decomposition and the
  collapse/crossing detector. The thermodynamics example above is the *method*,
  not a measurement — it becomes a measurement once `curvature.py` + `lens.py`
  put real numbers on each node and edge.
- No claim that any one ruler is privileged. The point is the *kit* and what each
  ruler uniquely reads.
