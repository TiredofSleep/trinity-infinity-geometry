## Tier discipline reminder

The framework's tier system:

- **PROVED** (Tier A) — machine-verified formal proofs in `02_results/` and `verification/`
- **STRUCTURAL** (Tier B) — rigorous derivation with named load-bearing identifications
- **EMPIRICAL** (Tier B-) — computational observation at the scale noted
- **OPEN** (Tier B/C) — precisely-stated hypothesis, not asserted
- **SPECULATIVE / Tier C** — *this directory* — interpretive material; honest about being interpretive

Reading this directory as if it were the framework's central content would be a misread. The central content is the algebra (`02_results/`) and the formal proofs (`03_canonical_reference/` + `verification/`). What's here is "after you have seen the math, here are some things that *might* be going on under it."

## Honest negatives

The framework has tried and failed at several things. These are documented in [`HONEST_NEGATIVES_AND_OPEN_FRONTIERS.md`](HONEST_NEGATIVES_AND_OPEN_FRONTIERS.md):

- Direct combinatorial bijection between the 32 divisors of Z/2310 and the 32 Pauli electron states — integer match real, structural bijection fails
- The fine-structure constant 1/α via `{±1, ±√7, ±π/7}` rational combinations — earlier attempts fail at ~12.6%
- F_p universality — only `p ∈ {7, 11}` preserve rank under the lift; other primes vary
- T\* = 5/7 as an algebraic theorem — six derivations converge but no single closed-form theorem produces it; treat as operational coherence threshold
- Eigenvalue-as-transcendental claims (the earlier "CL eigenvalues recover e, π, φ, ζ(3), Catalan G to 1%" only survives as 1% coincidences, not algebraic identities; per audit 2026-04-25)

The framework's tier discipline is designed to make these failures easy to surface and easy to correct. The discipline is not optional.
